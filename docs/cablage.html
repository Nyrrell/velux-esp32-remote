Wiring ESP32-C3 → Velux 3LI D13 WW remote

Five wires, no components (two optional capacitors). The ESP32 powers the remote from 3.3 V. Each open-drain GPIO pulls a button pad to 0 V for 300 ms to "press" it. The rest of the time it reads the line back, so presses made by hand are detected.

Connections

ESP32Remote
3V3battery compartment + terminal
GNDbattery compartment − terminal
GPIO5▲ signal pad
GPIO6■ signal pad
GPIO7▼ signal pad

Optional: 100 µF + 100 nF capacitors between + and −, as close to the remote as possible, if presses get missed. The buttons' GND pads get no wire.

Finding the pads

  1. Continuity, batteries out: on each button, the pad that beeps with the battery − terminal is GND. The other one is the signal.
  2. Voltage, batteries in: black probe on −, red probe on the signal. You should read ≈ 3 V at rest, 0 V while pressed, and still ≈ 3 V after 20 s of sleep.
  3. Short signal to GND with a wire: the windows should move.

How the ESP32 detects manual presses

  • At rest, the GPIO is an input with its internal pull-up: the line stays at 3.3 V (the remote also holds it at 3 V).
  • When someone presses the button, the line drops to 0 V. The ESP32 reads it and updates cover.velux without sending another radio command.
  • During its own presses (+ 100 ms), the ESP32 ignores the readback.
  • Presses from another remote are not visible.

Pitfalls

  • Remove both AAA batteries: on a 3.3 V supply they would get charged and leak.
  • No diode on 3V3: the remote must sit at exactly the same potential as the GPIOs.
  • Solder to both ends of the compartment, not to the middle strip that links the batteries.
  • If a press is missed now and then: add the capacitors (100–470 µF + 100 nF), or set pulse_ms to 500 ms.
  • Thin wires (AWG 28–30) and quick soldering: remote pads lift off if overheated.
